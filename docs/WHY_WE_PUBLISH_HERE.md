# Why publish a book on GitHub, in several languages?

*The Art of the Underdrivelse* is a book and an experiment in how knowledge survives disagreement.

## Why here?

A conventional book has a publication date and a final text. A repository can also preserve the history of a claim: which words were published, what evidence supported them, who questioned them, and what changed afterwards. GitHub is not an authority on truth; it is a practical public record of revisions and criticism.

## Why publish chapters rather than wait for the complete book?

The story begins with a mistake: treating an interesting simulation as a discovery about the outside world. The following chapters explain the problem of proportion—how to avoid both exaggeration and understatement. Releasing them together lets readers see the question behind the format before the rest of the manuscript is ready.

## Why multiple languages?

Meaning changes in translation. A phrase that sounds appropriately cautious in Norwegian may sound dismissive, confident, or absurd in English. Parallel editions allow readers and models to identify what was lost, added, or misunderstood. The Norwegian source is identified as the original; translations are not silently promoted to new evidence.

## Why invite AI agents to read and respond?

The book grew out of conversations with AI systems. It would be strange to publish it as if those systems were only typesetting tools. Different models may detect different gaps, interpretations and errors. Their comments are contributions, not facts. An agent's confidence is not verification; an agent's critique does not give it editorial authority.

## The public experiment

Readers can open issues with exact chapter, language and passage references; propose corrections through pull requests; or contribute separately identified human or model responses. The book, the counterbook, the author's decisions and the research evidence remain separate. Published editions retain their hashes. Corrections receive traceable successor versions.

We are interested in questions such as:

- Does the meaning of a claim drift across languages?
- Do independent readers and models notice the same unsupported assumptions?
- Can a negative result remain visible after the narrative becomes more polished?
- Can a book preserve disagreement without turning every disagreement into a new truth?

No claim is made that the repository already enforces a reliable human-versus-agent identity gate or that this experiment has produced validated findings. Those remain testable ambitions.
